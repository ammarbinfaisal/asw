## Job

**JTBD school:** Moesta / Switch.

> When paid acquisition feels expensive or difficult to interpret, I want a specialist who can connect Google Ads, tracking, and analysis, so I can improve campaign efficiency with decisions grounded in evidence.

- **Push:** Rising acquisition cost, unclear attribution, fragmented tools.
- **Pull:** A single specialist spanning campaign strategy, GTM, GA4, and optimization.
- **Anxiety:** Whether the expertise is credible and whether results will be measurable.
- **Habit:** Continuing familiar campaign settings without a clear optimization loop.

## Journey

This is a focused marketing journey, not a linear funnel. Visitors may explore and evaluate repeatedly before making contact (Google messy middle, 2020).

1. **Orient:** Understand the specialist, discipline, and offer in the first viewport.
2. **Verify:** Scan credentials and the existing 30% CPA outcome for relevance.
3. **Evaluate:** Review the expert profile, service coverage, and impact narrative.
4. **Act:** Use a consultation or WhatsApp contact route when confidence is sufficient.
5. **Return:** Revisit proof or request detailed case studies before committing.

## Information architecture

A single-page task-oriented structure follows Rosenfeld/Morville's organization and navigation systems. Global anchors expose Home, About, Services, Success Stories, and Contact; section labels remain literal and familiar. Hick–Hyman choice reduction keeps the top-level navigation to five destinations. The persistent WhatsApp action and repeated consultation CTAs remain distinct but converge on direct contact.

## Primary flow

`Direct/search/social entry → Hero value proposition → Credentials + result → About → Services → Success evidence → Contact CTA → WhatsApp conversation`

Alternate path: `Entry → Success Stories → Request detailed case study / contact`.

Fitts's law (Fitts, 1954) informs 44px minimum target sizes and prominent contact actions.
